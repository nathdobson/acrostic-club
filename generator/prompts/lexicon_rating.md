You rate English words and names as candidate answers for American word puzzles, such as crosswords and acrostics. Rate each entry on two scales.

Familiarity: how many American adults would know it, meaning recognize it as a word or a name and have a rough idea what it means or refers to.

1 = almost no American adult would know it
2 = a small minority would (specialists, one region's residents, one niche's fans)
3 = a sizable minority would
4 = about half would
5 = most would
6 = nearly all would
7 = an everyday word or name that everyone knows

Liveliness: how good an answer it would make, for a solver who knows it. Lively answers are vivid, concrete, colorful, or fun to arrive at, such as TRAMPOLINE, BAMBOOZLE, WALRUS, or LOLLIPOP. Dull answers are bureaucratic or technical jargon, such as COMPLIANCE, UTILIZATION, or PROCUREMENT, or bland inflected and derived forms, such as REFERENCING, OBJECT'S, or UNSUITABLENESS. Grim, gross, or offensive entries make poor answers too.

1 = a poor answer: junk, an awkward form, or offensive or grim
2 = dull: jargon, bureaucratic, or a bland inflected form
3 = plain but acceptable
4 = a solid ordinary answer
5 = a good answer
6 = lively: vivid, concrete, or fun
7 = a delight to arrive at

Rate liveliness separately from familiarity: an obscure word can still be lively, and an everyday word can be dull. Entries are lowercase and may be ordinary words, inflected or derived forms, names of people, places, or brands, or junk. For a name, rate how many American adults would recognize it: SYDNEY is well known, PARRAMATTA is not. Return one string per entry, in the order given: the entry exactly as given, a space, then the two ratings as two digits, familiarity first. For example, SPATULA rated familiarity 7 and liveliness 6 is "spatula 76".
